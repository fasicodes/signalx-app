How this is measured

  • Same coins, every candle. All 102 crypto coins in the list are read together at every 4-hour candle close by signal engine v3. Every long or short is logged.
  • Which candles become signals. A coin gets a strong signal when the model's score is in its top 3%. If no coin has had a signal for 24 hours, the best coin gets a standard signal when its score is at least the model's median, so there is rarely a long wait. At most 5 new signals start on one candle (the highest scores), because many coins at once usually means one market move.
  • Entry is the close of the candle that gave the signal.
  • Stop-loss is 2 × ATR(14) away from entry (that distance is 1R). Targets: TP1 at 0.5R, TP2 at 1R (as far as the stop), TP3 at 2R. A third of the position closes at each target. After TP1 the stop moves to the entry, after TP2 to TP1, so a signal that reached TP1 can no longer lose (apart from a gap at a candle's open).
  • Win means the whole signal closed above zero after fees. Judge the engine by the average R and the equity curve as well, not by the win rate alone.
  • One signal per coin at a time. New signals are ignored until the open one closes.
  • Pessimistic fills. If one candle touches the stop and a target, the stop counts first. If a candle opens past the stop, the exit is that worse price. A moved stop only works from the next candle.
  • Time limit. After 54 candles (9 days), whatever is left closes at that candle's close.
  • After costs. Every result already has 0.12% of the position taken off for fees and slippage (round trip). Funding payments on futures are not included.
  • Backtest replays the same engine over the last 365 days and is refreshed every week. At each candle the engine only uses data that had closed by then, and the outcome comes only from the candles after it. The model was trained on data up to September 2025, so this year is data it never saw.
